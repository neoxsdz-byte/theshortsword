# How Party Works (process and style)

From Claude's stored preferences and from the way the notes doc reads.

## Process, non-negotiable
1. Party sends a scene prompt describing what happens, usually including exact dialogue.
2. Claude **breaks the beats first**, plus a short critique of the prompt's storytelling: what's working, what isn't, what to steer toward or away from.
3. Party gives the all-clear.
4. Claude writes the scene.
5. **Never get ahead.** No invented plot, no preemptive scene extensions, no moving the narrative forward without being asked.

- **Dialogue Party supplies is not to be changed** in tone or content. Vocabulary can be adjusted to fit the period and theme, as long as it respects the speaker's personality.
- Critique the building; don't change things unilaterally.
- Corrections are surgical: fix only the flagged segment, never rewrite the surrounding material.
- Flag ambiguities and wait rather than making judgment calls.
- Where Party says "you have creative liberty", he means it, but only for the beat he named.
- Party corrects errors directly and without elaboration, and expects the correction acknowledged and the right answer, not a defence. He also expects to be challenged: "Fantastic questions, always challenge me like this from now on."
- Party values precision and geometric accuracy in the worldbuilding (the kingdom's layout is engineered, and the numbers matter).

## Style
- Spare, precise, dialogue-driven, grounded. Not ornate.
- Vinland Saga is the visual and tonal touchstone (the cover prompts say so explicitly: heavy ink, muted palette, cinematic framing).
- Game of Thrones naming sensibility. Islamic dua structure for prayers. Quranic/Biblical vagueness for scripture and myth.
- Deliberate repetition, mythic cadence in Caen's voice and fight-choreography pacing are intentional; keep them.
- The "Not X. X." fragment style stays.
- Critique should sound casual and human, not polished or AI-generated.

## Humanization pass (run per chapter during revision)
Identify recurring AI patterns across the whole chapter before rewriting, then produce the full rewrite as a markdown file. Best submitted a chapter at a time, or in pairs.

Patterns to eliminate:
- **"the specific [noun] of [noun/gerund]"** — the dominant tic; it appears dozens of times per chapter in the current draft and is the single biggest giveaway.
- "specific" as a filler qualifier anywhere.
- Em-dash explanatory clauses in nearly every paragraph.
- "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." tricolon used repetitively (as distinct from the intentional two-part fragment style, which stays).
- Redundant post-statement explanation: saying the thing, then explaining the thing.
- Over-nominalization of simple actions.
