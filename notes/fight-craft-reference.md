# Fight craft reference

Named fights worth studying, with what each one solves. Built for the choreography problem: keeping combat legible once power levels pass the point where a reader can measure anything.

**Verification status, 11 Oct 2026.** The first version of this file (commit `620fb3e`) was written from memory and had nine errors in it, some serious. This version has been checked:

- **Worm** — verified by reading the actual chapter text at parahumans.wordpress.com, which is free and author-published.
- **Everything else** — commercial manga and novels that can't legally be read here. Verified against publisher pages, Wikipedia, Coppermind and established fandom wikis. Chapter and volume numbers below are sourced, not recalled.
- Anything that could not be confirmed is in the **Unverified** block at the bottom. Don't trust those lines.

## Worm (Wildbow)

The closest structural cousin: street-level to world-killing entities, protagonist's power is bugs. Every fight is problem-solving on a clock. *Read from the source text.*

### Taylor vs Lung — Arc 1 *Gestation*, ch 1.03–1.06

The mechanism chapter is **1.04**. She can't touch his armoured, burning body, so she goes at the only wet unarmoured surface in reach — **eyes, eyelids, face** — driving venomous insects at them while ants and spiders work elsewhere. He answers each wave by exploding into flame and escalating: metal plating, full-body fire, a plated spine, a foot taller. **She fails alone and retreats.** In 1.05 she adds pepper spray and Tattletale tallies the cumulative damage; Taylor says her bugs are useless against him now, and **Bitch's dogs do the physical takedown.** 1.06 opens with Lung already unconscious. The final blow is never shown.

**Craft:** the opponent's escalation clock turns slow-accumulating damage into a race. And the protagonist is allowed to be *one component* of the win rather than the win.

### Leviathan — Arc 8 *Extermination*, 8.1–8.8

Defence clearest in **8.4**, ending in **8.5**, the cost in **8.7–8.8**. Heroes and villains fight under a truce — about fifty capes, only ~fifteen able to engage directly. Kaiser cages its limbs in metal while telekinetics hold the bars; Armsmaster repurposes grenades as proximity mines using Dragon's sensor data; Narwhal opens a forcefield gap for Kid Win to shoot through; Eidolon freezes the coastal water to blunt the waves. All of it coordinated by **PRT armbands, which double as the casualty feed.** Taylor's job is sensing.

**Leviathan is not killed — it's driven off,** by Scion. Named dead: Manpower, Aegis, Fenja, Kaiser, Escutcheon, Herald. Armsmaster loses an arm. The memorial in 8.8 carries ~38 names.

**Craft:** the armband casualty feed is the device. It externalises attrition so the reader feels every death without the POV having to witness one.

### Taylor vs Alexandria — Arc 22 *Cell*, **ch 22.4**

Alexandria is invulnerable down to her organs. She escalates by abducting Undersiders one at a time at five-minute intervals to break Taylor. Taylor's answer isn't force: bugs swarm nose and mouth, **spiders glue themselves and other insects into her throat with silk**, and when she coughs more get into the lungs and form a sealing layer. Invulnerable tissue can't be cut — but **the volume of an airway can be occupied.** She suffocates. Taylor simultaneously kills Tagg with hornets and widow bites.

**Craft:** beat an invulnerable body by attacking a **space** rather than a tissue. Find the need the invulnerability clause doesn't cover, and fill it.

### Behemoth / New Delhi — Arc 24 *Crushed*, 24.1–24.5, resolved in Interlude 24

24.1: Behemoth has already wrecked the city; heroes raise a metal spire as a lightning rod while the wounded are evacuated on sleds. **24.4: Phir Sē's accumulated-light strike strips roughly 80% of Behemoth's body — and he regenerates and keeps walking.** Retreat is ordered. Regent dies, with Rime and Ligeia. In Interlude 24 Chevalier's cannonblade takes a leg and cuts into the core, Alexandria tears off an arm, and **Scion kills him.**

**Craft:** the 80%-and-regenerating beat. *A plan that works perfectly and still isn't enough* is more frightening than a plan that fails.

### Echidna — Arcs 18 *Queen* → 19 *Scourge*, killed in **19.7**

She absorbs capes and vomits out **hostile clones that keep the original's memories** — twisted versions, so a clone of a Triumvirate member is a Triumvirate-level enemy. In 19.6 the Eidolon-clone kills Myrddin. Miss Militia runs the overall response while Chevalier, the Wards and the Undersiders split into pursuit groups and villains are recruited mid-crisis. She survives being sheared in half. **Sundancer kills her** in 19.7, growing her miniature sun over her while Alexandria pins her.

**Craft:** every captured ally converts into an enemy of equal power. **Attrition that compounds instead of subtracting** — losing a fight makes the next fight harder.

## Hunter × Hunter (Togashi)

Nen's rules are published to the reader and never change; fights are won by exploiting constraints.

| Fight | Chapters | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Kurapika vs Uvogin** | **79–81** (vol 9) | Power bought with restriction. Chain Jail works only on Phantom Troupe members, enforced by a Judgment Chain around his own heart — breaking the limitation kills him. |
| **Gon vs Hisoka** | **61–63** (vol 7) | A fight that's a lesson being administered. The loser gains more than the winner. |
| **Team vs Razor** | **161–168** (vols 16–17) | Rules-as-arena: lethal dodgeball. An arbitrary constraint makes a fight *more* tense. |
| **Zeno vs Neferpitou** | **262–264** | Veteran assassin reading a monster's capabilities in real time. |
| **Netero vs Meruem** | **287–298** (vols 27–28) | Humanity's peak against something better. 100-Type Guanyin Bodhisattva, then Zero Hand, then **Netero detonates the Poor Man's Rose by piercing his own heart** in 298. A fight deliberately lost in order to win. |
| **Gon vs Neferpitou** | **305–307** (vol 29) | Transformation as horror. The price is paid and never refunded. |
| **Hisoka vs Chrollo** | **351–357** (vol 34), Succession Contest arc | Preparation, environment weaponisation, and conditions that keep operating after a fighter is dead. |

**Steal:** the narrator stopping a fight to state a technique's conditions. Tension goes *up*, because the reader can now do the arithmetic on who loses.

## Jujutsu Kaisen (Akutami)

Power is purchased with restriction — the **binding vow** (*shibari*): a self-imposed limitation traded for strength, with the gain lost if it's broken.

| Fight | Chapters | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Jujutsu Kaisen 0** | 4-chapter prequel volume | A complete arc in one volume. **Not** a Yuta-vs-Geto duel — Geto clashes with Maki, Panda and Toge, Yuta releases Rika, Geto escapes injured and is executed by Gojo later. |
| **Yuji & Todo vs Hanami** | Goodwill Event arc | Two fighters synchronising mid-fight. Rare and hard to write. |
| **Gojo vs Jogo** | Shibuya Incident | Demonstrating an absurd power gap while keeping it readable. |
| **Sukuna vs Mahoraga** | Shibuya Incident | An opponent that adapts to any technique. Forces a win by category change, not force. |
| **Maki vs the Kukuru Unit** | **ch 150**, Perfect Preparation arc (144–158) | Someone with no cursed energy beating sorcerers. Constraint as identity. The arc also holds Maki vs Ogi Zenin and vs Naoya. |
| **Gojo vs Sukuna** | **223–236**, Shinjuku Showdown | The biggest fight in the work. Gojo dies in 236. Ch 221 is where it's agreed, 223 where it starts. Study the binding vows declared aloud mid-combat. |

**Steal:** binding vows — the worked example of the one-constraint-that-never-bends problem flagged as S5 in `structural-critique.md`.

## Berserk (Miura)

Tonally nearest to what you're writing: grounded brutality escalating into Apostles and the Godhand. Same lineage as Vinland Saga, going where your book is going.

| Fight | Volume | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Guts vs Bazuso** | **vol 4**, "Golden Age (3)" | The establishing duel. Scale set by one man against one man. |
| **Guts vs Griffith, first duel** | **vol 4** | Griffith wins and conscripts him. |
| **Guts vs Griffith, second duel** | **vol 8**, after the queen's funeral | **The same fight twice with different outcomes.** The second means something only because the first exists. |
| **Guts vs Nosferatu Zodd** | **vol 5** | Meeting something that cannot be beaten; survival as the only available victory. (Later clash in vol 22.) |
| **The Hundred Man Slayer** | **vol 7**, ep 21 | Guts stays behind so Casca can escape and takes ~100 Tudor soldiers alone in a forest, during the Golden Age campaign. Attrition choreography with exhaustion tracked honestly. |
| **The Eclipse** | **vols 12–13** | Helplessness at scale. Nobody wins anything; still the best sequence in the work. |
| **Guts vs Grunbeld** | **vols 26–27**, Millennium Falcon | Post-escalation Guts against armour that negates him — and the Berserker Armor's debut mid-fight. Power creep handled by changing the problem. |

**Steal:** weight. Wounds persist across volumes, fatigue is never forgotten for narrative convenience.

## Vagabond (Inoue)

Almost no power scaling. Here for the opposite reason to Cradle.

| Fight | What it solves |
|---|---|
| **Musashi vs Inshun** | He faces Hōzōin Inshun **twice** — first he confronts his fear of death and runs; in the rematch he briefly overpowers him and **spares him**. A duel resolved by not killing. |
| **Musashi vs Denshichiro** | A rematch granted a year earlier, decided in a single exchange after an enormous build, with serious consequences for Musashi afterward. |
| **The 70 Yoshioka** | Musashi alone against ~70 men, kills them all, takes a severe foot wound from a dying opponent. Attrition, terrain, and the mind going somewhere else. |

**Important:** *Vagabond* **never depicts the Kojiro duel.** The manga has been on indefinite hiatus since 21 May 2015, last installment ch 327, 37 volumes, no ending published. The only Musashi–Kojiro swordplay shown is a playful mock-fight around a snowman. The first version of this file claimed the final duel as a thing to study; it doesn't exist.

**Steal:** the moment before contact. Inoue spends more on distance, breathing and stance than on the strike.

## Cradle (Will Wight)

Twelve books, boy-who-can't-fight to continent-breakers, legible throughout because of an ordered advancement ladder — **Copper → Iron → Jade → Gold**, up through Underlord and beyond — so the gap between two fighters is always countable.

| Fight | Where | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Lindon vs Jai Long** | **book 4, *Skysworn*** (set up through *Blackflame*, bk 3) | The first real duel with a stated rank gap. A whole book of preparation; the fight is the payment. |
| **The Uncrowned King tournament** | **book 7, *Uncrowned*** | Highest fight-density volume. A dozen duels, each a different problem. |
| **Wintersteel finale** | **book 8, *Wintersteel*** | Where the scale breaks open and the ladder has to stretch. |
| **Dreadgod fights** | ***Bloodline*** (9), ***Reaper*** (10), ***Dreadgod*** (11) | City-and-continent scale. Watch what he shows breaking and what he refuses to describe. |

Full order: Unsouled, Soulsmith, Blackflame, Skysworn, Ghostwater, Underlord, Uncrowned, Wintersteel, Bloodline, Reaper, Dreadgod, **Waybound** (bk 12, 2023, the finale).

**Steal:** the ladder. A one-rank upset thrills, a four-rank upset terrifies, and the reader knows the difference without being told. Your book has nothing like this.

## The Green Bone Saga (Fonda Lee)

Lower ceiling, best prose choreography on the list. If the problem is sentence-level — how to write a fast exchange so a reader can see it — read her and nothing else here.

| Scene | Where | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Lan vs Gam** | *Jade City* | A genuine formal duel, fought to win jade. **This is the duel in book 1**, not the Gont Asch confrontation. |
| **Hilo vs Gont Asch** | *Jade City*, climax | **Not a duel — a swarming.** Hilo walks into Mountain territory expecting to die, beats the first few men sent at him, then is overwhelmed; he calls to **Anden**, who takes his hidden jade and freezes Gont Asch, and they kill him together. Anden falls into a coma. Study it as *a fight the protagonist loses and survives because of someone else*. |
| **Shae vs Ayt Mada** | ***Jade War*** (bk 2) | Shae challenges Ayt to a duel, **loses, and is nearly killed** — then regains public respect for having fought. A duel whose point is the defeat. |

**Steal:** her sentence rhythm in close combat. Short clauses, concrete verbs, position restated often enough that the reader never loses the room.

## The Stormlight Archive (Sanderson)

Mostly for one principle the author states outright: **a magic system's ability to resolve a conflict satisfyingly is proportional to how well the reader understands it.** That sentence is the whole answer to S5.

| Fight | Where | What it solves |
|---|---|---|
| **Szeth kills Gavilar** | *The Way of Kings*, prologue | Opens the series by showing an unknown system doing impossible things — and it works *because* it's the opening, before any rules are owed. |
| **Adolin's four-on-one duel** | *Words of Radiance* ch 61 (set up ch 22) | The best small fight Sanderson has written. Relis brings three others, Renarin is used as leverage, Kaladin ends it. Clear rules, clear stakes, no cosmic scale. |
| **Kaladin vs Szeth** | *Words of Radiance* ch 86 | Two users of the same system at different levels of understanding. Part of the climactic sequence, running alongside the Battle of Narak. |
| **Battle of Thaylen Field** | *Oathbringer* ch 115–120 | Enormous scale resolved by a character decision rather than a power increase. Dalinar attains Unity in 117–118. |

## Reverend Insanity

Xianxia, extremely dark, protagonist wins while outclassed, almost always by scheming. Unfinished, and the protagonist is genuinely monstrous. **Not verified** — individual fights are deliberately not named here because the sources available are unreliable.

What's worth studying is the **plan-execution structure**: Fang Yuan enters nearly every confrontation having already arranged the terrain, the information and the opponent's assumptions. The combat is the collection, not the contest.

**Steal:** winning before the fight starts. Directly applicable to Caen, and to Ryn.

## Cross-cutting principles

1. **Give the reader a unit.** Ranks, technique categories, something countable. Without a measuring stick, levelling a mountain and levelling a city read identically.
2. **Cost is the only tension left when nobody can be hurt.** Time, terrain, bystanders, a technique that can't be used twice, a vow that kills you if you break it.
3. **Show scale through what breaks, never through adjectives.**
4. **Geography matters more at high power, not less.**
5. **State the rules, then have someone win by using them.** Every verified fight above does this. None are won by a number going up.
6. **One clear idea per exchange.** Complexity across the fight, simplicity inside each beat.
7. **The protagonist doesn't have to land the final blow.** Taylor doesn't beat Lung. Hilo doesn't beat Gont Asch. Netero doesn't survive Meruem. The fight still belongs to them.

## What *The Shortsword* already does right

**Ch 21, the Blighted Murel.** Three rules stated — absorbs bullets, re-fuses when split, smothers fire with acid — and the win comes from Mara's fire ring, one tactic that beats all three at once. Varen's strength isn't what ends it.

That's the template, written before this file existed. The risk going up the power curve isn't that the choreography can't be done; it's that when the numbers get large enough it stops *feeling* necessary, and the fight becomes a trade of enormous blows instead of a problem being solved.

## Unverified — do not rely on these

- **The "rice field" setting** for the 70-man Yoshioka fight. The fight is confirmed; a published review describes "muddy fields." No source confirms a rice paddy.
- **The spear as the weapon** in the Musashi–Inshun bouts. The Hōzōin school's spears are documented; the weapon in those specific fights isn't stated in any source found.
- **"Clean-blade duel"** as Green Bone Saga terminology. No authoritative source uses the phrase.
- **Cradle's ladder as a *publicly displayed* ranking.** The ordered stages are confirmed. The badges in book 1 mark combat discipline, not advancement rank — don't claim a visible rank-badge system.
