# Structural critique

Story-level problems with the draft as it stands at Ch 25. Not continuity errors — those live in `continuity-flags.md`. These are craft calls, and they're the author's to make.

Raised 10 October 2026, from the full read of Ch 1–25. Each entry says what the problem is, where it shows, and what it costs. No fixes applied. Nothing here has been actioned.

---

## S1. The protagonist is absent from the climax of his own act

**Where:** Ch 22, 23, 24, 25 — the Ordenmere siege. Varen's last appearance is Ch 21.

Four consecutive chapters with no Varen, no Mara, no Ryn. The POV is Vorne, Seris, Cedric, Silas, Highmore, Emmeline and the clerics.

Cutting away from a protagonist is normal. The problem is *which* four chapters. **The Godless Hand attack Ordenmere** — the faction that burned Varen's village, the reason the character exists, the engine of the whole book — and Varen is in a swamp outside the Murel village running an escort job for six gold.

Two possibilities and the text doesn't settle which:

- **Deliberate irony.** He misses it. Then the moment he learns has to be the largest beat in the arc, and Ch 19–21 should be seeding the near-miss so the reader feels it coming.
- **Accident.** Then Ch 22–25 is happening to the wrong cast, and the act climax is spending its weight on characters the protagonist has never met.

Current reading: accident, because nothing in Ch 19–21 points him back toward the city.

**Cost:** the single strongest convergence the book has set up — his vow meeting its target — is sitting unused while the climax is given to a parallel cast.

## S2. Varen's goal hasn't advanced since Ch 4

Stated Ch 1 ("I'll burn them"), confirmed Ch 3, explained Ch 4, restated Ch 19. Twenty-one chapters, same unadvanced want.

He gets one real lead — **Ch 14: "Do you think the church would know where she is"** — and doesn't follow it. He takes a monster contract (Ch 10), then an escort job (Ch 17).

**Compare Sera**, introduced in Ch 12, half the book in, with a want of near-identical shape (find the truth, find Providence):

| | |
|---|---|
| Ch 12 | faith breaks at the border; "Where are you" |
| Ch 13 | walks to the compound without knowing why |
| Ch 15 | makes the deal with Seris; pays with her habit |
| Ch 18 | gets the truth; it dismantles the church's doctrine |
| Ch 22 | finds the erased history |

Five chapters, five escalations, each one costing her something.

**Cost:** Sera is currently the better-built protagonist in the manuscript. Varen has a want and a sequence of monsters; she has a want, an obstacle, a price and progress.

## S3. Caen is doing Varen's work for him

| Where | What Caen does |
|---|---|
| Ch 16 | adds two logs to their fire, unseen |
| Ch 18 | turns Lyris into a double agent, so the threat never reaches Varen |
| Ch 19 | nudges the embers into the shape of the burning village and the verse, triggering the vow |
| Ch 21 | steers the World Feeders into the well, which breaks the seal and starts the Blighted Murel fight |

So Varen's hardest fight to date is one his mentor arranged, against a monster his mentor released, after his mentor manufactured his emotional state the chapter before. He wins it — the fire ring, the nerve cluster, the flaming sword all work — but he caused none of it, and doesn't know.

Dramatic irony is fine. The problem is that it's every time.

**Varen has agency in combat and nowhere else.** Across Ch 1–21 he makes about two decisions that change his own path: following Caen in Ch 1, and "Fine" to Mara in Ch 10. Everything after is response to a situation someone else built.

## S4. Mara's stated age doesn't match what the book asks of her

**Stated:** nine years old (Ch 6).

**What she does:** haggles a trader down with real authority; delivers the book's best speech on bounty economics ("It pays the most because it kills the most… that's a warning with a number on it", Ch 10); runs rooftops mid-combat against a creature taller than the houses (Ch 21); devises the tactic that wins the chapter (Ch 21).

The competence is the best thing about her and shouldn't be reduced. The age is the part that doesn't hold.

Secondary effect: **Varen's sister was seven.** At nine, Mara is almost the same child, which flattens the ache every time he looks at her. At twelve or thirteen she's past his sister — worse for him, better for the book.

---

## S5. The magic system's rules change four times in ten chapters

| Ch | New rule |
|---|---|
| 15 | divine energy is renamed **mana**; the **cowl** is the fundamental |
| 18 | purification was never divine — it's **healing magic that predates the church** |
| 22 | it isn't quantity, it's **quality** |
| 24 | the **Wand of Rime** "breaks every fundamental rule" |

Each reveal works on its own. Stacked, they teach the reader that the rules don't hold — and once a reader learns that, fights stop being tense, because any new rule can arrive to settle them.

**What's needed:** one constraint that never bends. The cowl is the obvious candidate — whatever else turns out to be a lie, mana in the body behaves one way, always. Right now even that is unstable: Sera's is viscous and nobody can copy it.

## Where the draft is strongest

Recorded because it tells the author what not to touch, and because it's the standard the rest is being held to.

**Ch 22–25, the siege.** Four locations cross-cut, every faction paying a cost, and Cedric measuring Vorne for three chapters before the hidden plain-steel sword comes out from behind the quartzite vambrace. Nothing in Ch 1–21 is built this tightly, which is why the Varen problem shows at all.
