## THE DEEP HISTORY (mostly unknown in-world)

The chain, as the author has worked it out:

1. **The crack.** A crack between this world and another dimension has existed since the universe formed. It leaks the **mana** this world runs on. Nobody knows this.
2. **Auric dragons.** They had the best relationship with mana that has ever existed; their life force merged with it, "as if it was engraved in their souls." **They created magic.** Everything anyone casts today is a branch of dragon magic, Providence's flames included.
3. **The gods** were mortals who took a shortcut: they **usurped auric souls** instead of developing understanding the way the dragons did. They were real, there were many of them, and they could die.
4. **Humans** learned magic from the dragons, and the **four types** (fire, water, wind, earth; plus lightning, light, ice, and so on in practice) are just the first human users' interpretations. The raw underlying form of magic is still **unnamed**.
5. **The crusade.** Yharim, the boy from the magma, and Yharon killed the gods one after another. The **Devourer of Gods** devoured the auric souls; Yharim believed it was destroying them, but it was absorbing their power.
6. **The wound.** Two gods' remains were left where they fell. They became **the Corruption** (this continent) and **the Crimson**; the "**crawling flesh**" in the Scorched Expanse is the same phenomenon from another god.
7. **The erasure.** Someone has spent centuries systematically removing this history. The word "dragon" survives in our alphabet with its meaning stripped out. Seris suspects an institution that outlives its people.

### The Providence Calendar (locked)
- 23 BP: Xeroc's betrayal
- Year 0: Providence becomes a goddess
- Year 14: Yharim born
- Year 26: Yharim thrown into the magma; Yharon hatches
- Year 31: the crusade begins
- Year 87: the crusade ends; the New Age begins
- **Year 523, 14 November: story present**
- Structure: a ten-month Roman-style calendar (Martius through December, 304 days) plus an unnamed winter period.

### Exceptional mortals
Life force sets a person's ceiling, and it can push different aspects: body, intelligence, or understanding of mana.
- **Calamitas** — reached dragon-level understanding as a mortal; created brimstone flame to rival Providence's flame (or the dragon of flames).
- The big five: **Supreme Calamitas, Skeletron, Statis, Baelor, Permafrost** (author's shorthand), plus **Draedon**, whose life force pushed his intelligence to unimaginable levels.
- Planned for the same tier: **Sera**, **Aria** (dryad), and the **lunatic cultist** later on.
- **Varen**: body. **Ryn**: intelligence (and the philosopher stone will carry the rest).

### The philosopher stone
- Only **one** exists. Not a physical fragment of the crack; it's crystallized pure mana formed from excess leakage, and it took **millions of years** to form one small stone.
- Formed in **the Shimmer**. Held by **Silva, the first dryad** (dryads are nearly as old as the dragons), the only one who understood what it was. Lost in **the Abyss** when she died; the stone has transmuted the area around her body into a small closed environment there.
- Nobody knows it exists, which is why **Ryn finds it early without looking for it**, and it unlocks an alchemy that ignores this world's rules.

## MAGIC SYSTEM

### Terminology (current, being applied going forward)
| Old term | New term | Who knows |
|---|---|---|
| mana | **arcana** | what every mage calls it |
| life force | **anima** | secret; only the Devourer's envoys and Caen |
| the root of mana | **the Aether** | nobody; Seris suspects it and calls it "the root" |
Chapters 1–25 as written still use "mana" and "life force"; the church still says "divine energy" or "Providence's blessing". This gets fixed in revision.

### Arcana (mana)
- Sits in the space around everyone and is absorbed through the skin. Capability depends on the **relationship** between the caster and the mana, that is, how much the mind and body understand and accept it.
- Church method: absorb, push out through the palms. Seris's method: absorb, **let it stay**, and distribute it through the whole body.
- Anything conjured from mana **returns to mana**; nothing elemental is permanent. A water mage can't drink his own magic.
- **Quantity vs quality:** everyone measures how much mana they can hold, because that's all they know how to measure. Sera's is a different grade of the same substance: thick, **viscous**, slow to dissipate. This is the same quality the ancient rune-makers must have used.

### The cowl
The visible field of arcana held through the whole body. Its colour and behaviour show the caster's **nature**, and its quality shows their character. Years of casting through the hands leaves a groove that has to be unlearned.
Training ladder as Seris teaches it: hold the cowl → hold it evenly → hold it effortlessly → **funnel** (release outward while the intake continues, so the cycle never breaks) → manifest the element → **shift nature (Convergence)**.
- **Convergence** is not element-swapping. It's when someone understands arcana well enough that they **stop seeing it in the simplified forms ordinary people use**. The four types are a human interpretation, so a converged mage is operating one level below the labels and can therefore express any of them. Seris has it, and even she isn't deep enough to touch the root arcana or the Aether.
- Using another element without full Convergence is possible but takes years (Seris demonstrates wind and fire in both hands). Outside her own nature she is weaker.

### Healing / purification
- The church's "purification" is a branch of **healing magic**, which predates the church, has nothing to do with Providence, and is one of the hardest forms of mana manipulation there is. Anyone can learn it regardless of nature, but it takes years and many fail.
- It works on living tissue at the level of its smallest parts: distinguish damaged from healthy, instruct, sustain the instruction without disrupting what still works.
- Sera trained from 6 to 16 before she was trusted at the border.
- "If it can heal it can also destroy."

### Anima (life force)
- Distinct from arcana. Everyone has one; almost nobody knows it exists. **Anima is not just life force — it is the individual's existence itself.** It sets the limits of the vessel: body, intelligence, understanding.
- **Nature comes from anima.** A mage's element isn't chosen or trained into them; it's an expression of what they are. Sera's arcana is a product of her anima the same way Ryn's intelligence is a product of his.
- **Understanding and anima are separate axes.** Sera has extraordinary anima and almost no understanding of arcana, only the church's false teaching. Seris is the reverse.
- **Spending anima costs lifespan, and it comes back slowly.** Rough scale (provisional, author may change it): if Silas burned about five years of his remaining life in that fight, recovering those five years would have taken him roughly a year.
- **Spend past what you have left and you die.** There is no reserve below the reserve.
- **Everyone has anima from the start.** What's exceptional about Varen isn't that his was somehow already finished at fourteen; it's the **growth**. His and the other protagonists' anima grows bigger and faster than a normal person's.
- **Harvesting kills.** Feeding cultivated anima to the Devourer is not taking something from a person, it is taking the person. That's the point of the Order.
- Can be read by those with the awareness (envoys). It has age, density and signature, so it identifies people. Anima from a worn object (the wolf hide) seeps faintly into the wearer's signature over years.
- **Non-envoys can sense it partially.** Vorne and Varen both register power in a person or a creature; neither can read true anima, at least not yet in the story.
- The Order cultivates exceptional anima and feeds it to the Devourer.
- Varen's reads as **blue fire**, already dense and refined far past his age, with a ceiling no envoy could find. Lyris describes it as "formed", but that's her reading of a fourteen-year-old whose anima is growing at a rate she has no reference for, not anima that arrived finished.
- **Summons feed on the summoner's anima**, not mana, and damage to a summon is paid by the summoner.

### Summoning
- **Summoners don't create anything.** Minions are pulled out of **another dimension that hasn't been named in the story yet**.
- The mechanism is **runic magic**: the summoner **binds his soul to the minion as a contract**, and that bind *is* the rent. It can be broken.
- This is **not** what happened between Yharim and Yharon. Yharon isn't from another dimension — his species ruled this world at one point — and that was a merging, not a contract.
- **The minion is a slave.** Binding one and keeping it in check takes a certain amount of power, which is the natural cap: Silas can't just summon something all-powerful, because he couldn't hold it.
- **Minions can't really die.** While summoned they regenerate, using the summoner's anima to do it. Kill the summoner and they simply return to their dimension.
- So a fight against a summoner is never about destroying the summon. It's about the summoner's clock or reaching the summoner, which is exactly how Ch 24–25 plays: nobody kills the toad, Berrick shoots Silas.
- **The cultist (future protagonist) uses the same mechanic as Silas.** He isn't a different tier of summoner; he's simply more powerful, so he binds stronger things.

### Runes and enchanted artifacts
- A script in our own letters that nobody can read, from before every current religion, treated with religious reverence in the fragments that survive.
- Runes **store and self-supply mana**: some pull it from the ambient air, others convert it into the effect. No operator needed, unlike the church's **pylons** (kingdom-to-kingdom travel, reserved for the important and the connected), which need people feeding them constantly.
- Seris's exact replica of the lantern fails. Her working theory now: the makers used mana of a different **quality**.
- Known artifacts: the **self-powered lantern** (its runes redraw the pieces that have crumbled away), the **Wand of Rime** (Emmeline's iced branch: ice erupts simultaneously from every point and leaves real moisture; "breaks every fundamental rule"), the **four-page fire book** (fire that clings like oil and eats the mana of a barrier; the pages turn themselves and the runes rearrange between shots), and **Silas's broom** (loses weight when fed mana). Note: project memory calls the wand the "Wand of Frosting"; the manuscript names it the **Wand of Rime**.
- A few artifacts sit in the capital castle that almost nobody is permitted to know about.

### Rifles are not magic
Ryn's weapons take no mana and don't respond to it, which is exactly what makes them terrifying and unclassifiable to mages. Silas fed his mana into a stolen rifle and got nothing.

## ORDENMERE

A perfectly engineered kingdom shaped like a plus sign, about 15 × 15 km overall.
- **Five square districts**, each 5 × 5 km: West, North, East, South, and the **Capital** dead centre.
- **Three triangular passage zones** hang off three outer diagonal corners (SW, SE, NE), about 12.5 km² each, walled, cramped, and poor: the slums. The NW corner has no passage, just wall.
- One thick fortified outer wall with crenellations, defence towers at every outer corner, and watch towers every ~100 m.
- **Three ways in:** two gates on the West district (north wall and west wall) and the **Bridge Gate** on the East district's east face.
- **The Great River** (Nile-sized, ~1.65 km wide) runs along the whole eastern edge. **Lord Shaemus Bridge** is fixed stone arches the whole way except one liftable section at the wall. The far bank is wilderness.
- **The Royal Road is a spiral**: in through the west, through West → South → East → North, then into the Capital through its only gate on the north side. Everyone reaching the Capital crosses all four districts first. **The kingdom is the security system.**
- The Capital has no outer wall, no river access and no windows on the world. It holds the Royal Palace and the **Church of Providence**, the grandest cathedral in the kingdom; each district has a branch chapel in the same style.
- District character: **West** loud and mercantile (and the most militarized, with the western brigade compound against the wall); **North** forges and smoke; **East** docks and fish; **South** fields and windmills.
- Gate numbering used in the project map doc (reference labels, not in-world names) is in `claude/ordenmere-map.md` in the project.

**Other places named:** Sundermere, Miredan, Ilyrath (kingdoms whose libraries Seris has read), Varell (northern domain, ~230 leagues away, the pilgrims' cover story), Oakhaven (House Highmore), the Scorched Expanse / desert continent (crawling flesh), the jungle where Yharim withdrew.
**Continents (from project memory):** Aldenor (largest, European weather, holds Ordenmere), Gor'zhal (jungle, home of the lizard-folk **Lihzard**), Ashar / the Scorched Expanse (desert, Arabian-themed), Borealis (ice, Eskimo-themed). The world is large; it isn't an island.

## THE CORRUPTION (the rot) AND ITS CREATURES

- A dead god's flesh and blood, spreading where it fell. It advances, learns and adapts, and it moves underground where purification can't reach. Purification wins centimeters a morning while it takes meters below. Forty years ago the border was three weeks' travel west of Ordenmere's wall; now it's hours. It will reach the walls.
- Signs: purple veining in rock, grass that is green and wrong, trees replaced from the inside, mineral weight in the air, and a standing grey-purple fog over the deep regions. It consumes evidence: bodies, tracks, whole sites, in days.
- The church calls it the **Unmaking** and teaches that it's divine punishment or a trial that Providence opposes. This is invention that grew from a footnote into the centrepiece of the doctrine.

**Creatures met so far**
- **Corrupters** (Ch 14, 17): three times a direwolf, hide with no fur, no eyes (smooth skin that pulses when reading), rows of oversized claws, pure muscle. Hunt by **sound**, feel no pain, coordinate as a pack through a shared link; a strike at the base of the skull deafens one and cuts it out of the link, and the others tear it apart. Silent for their size. Six of them killed by Varen (with Ryn's last two).
- **World Feeders** (Ch 12, 19, 21): burrowing worms with a ring of spinning teeth around nothing, acid-slicked skin that both lubricates and dissolves. They **only move forward** and hunt by **vibration**: "It only finds us when we move." The acid gasses stone on contact and melts steel. Caen's sword is untouched by it.
- **Blighted Murel** (Ch 21): twenty farmed Murelings, sealed in their enclosures when the village was abandoned, acid-melted their way out through stone, met, and **fused** into one mass over two years in a well. Taller than a house, with a dark **nerve cluster** at its core that makes twenty into one. Navigates by chemical scent; its overriding drive is **reabsorbing its own mass**, which is why it hunts the stolen crystal past everything else. Absorbs bullets. Burns readily, then sheds burning gel deliberately to survive. Killed by destroying the nerve cluster while trapped in fire.
- **Murelings** (uncorrupted): docile, slow, farmed for gel, need lots of water (they take microorganisms and minerals from it), merge on contact once grown. Their gel is flammable; crystallized, it flows under slow pressure and shatters under shock, which is the wrong way round for what Ryn needs. **Blighted** Murel gel is the material he went to get.

## FACTIONS AND RELIGION

- **The Church of Providence.** Providence, the **Profaned Goddess**. The border orders take in orphans, forbid them to learn each other's names, bury the dead without ceremony, and send the next team out in three days. Nobody asks where an orphan went. Prayers are built on the situational structure of Islamic dua (author's note), and the vagueness is deliberately Quranic/Biblical.
- **The Godless Hand.** No self-given name. Doctrine: the gods were corrupt, the crusade proved it, worship is corruption, and the fire should keep burning. They travel as individuals, a philosophy with legs, and they burn villages that still practise. One of them burned Varen's village. Verses used so far:
  - *"Thus it is written in the ash of the old gods. Those who kneel before false divinity shall know the fire that does not distinguish. Their flesh shall bear witness to what their faith could not. Flesh that burns in our fire burns clean. We are its mercy. We do not cease."*
  - *"They made idols of the stolen. What they worshipped held no power over what comes after. The false light fails at the threshold. Punishment receives what devotion could not."*
  - Farewell: *"Let the deep void claim you, brother. We will meet again in the eldritch."*
- **The Order of the Devourer.** 23 Guides/envoys in human form, each embedded in a region, who identify exceptional anima, cultivate it, and harvest it for the Devourer. They meet in a void that takes the shape of whatever intention occupies it. Caen is the senior. The **Weaver** supervises the Order but is not in Caen's chain of authority.
- **The nobility**: Houses Telvaris (Percival III), Astorel, Highmore of Oakhaven, Vorenthal, Thessaly, Arenthios.

## TECHNOLOGY (Ryn's line)

- **Revolver:** six-chamber rotating cylinder, blued steel, four-inch octagonal barrel, wood grip fitted with brass pins. Based on an 1847 Colt Walker.
- **Rifle:** bolt-channel action with a magazine, spring-loaded; cratered a compound wall. Recoil nearly floors an untrained soldier.
- **Repeating crossbow:** all-metal, ten-bolt staggered magazine, two springs on alternating tracks so the loading arm resets while the prod fires. Fire rate limited only by trigger pulls. Sold as schematics; the military blacksmiths mass-produced these fast (the rifles came in threes).
- **Ammunition:** brass case, propellant compound packed to a specific density, projectile, primer. Built on **infernal suevite** (volcanic glass from the Pyre's deepest reaches, formed where brimstone and rock fused), acid wood bark, anechoic coating, and a viscous gel. Seris identified suevite by smell. **Incendiary rounds** improvised in the field.
- Open problems in Ryn's journal: **recoil** (needs a non-Newtonian material, soft at rest and rigid under shock; blighted Murel gel is the candidate) and the **escapement contact wear** in Aldric's clock.
- Materials he trades in: Crimson-creature vertebrae (dangerous to harvest, confiscated at borders), vile mushrooms, restoration potions (20 silver a vial, common).
- Economy: 1 gold = 100 silver = 10,000 copper. A writ redeemable for 50+ gold at any chartered exchange house. The rifle deal: 20 gold (crossbow schematics), 1 platinum (rifle), 100 rounds up front, 30 silver per round after.

## Corrections to the older `claude/magic-system.md`
That doc is still broadly right, but three details are wrong:
1. It was **Lyris**, not the Weaver, who read Varen's anima at fourteen and again in the inn. The Weaver only heard her report.
2. Silas's summoning circle appears **above his head** (and a second one on the ground for the toad), not on his forehead.
3. Sera's nature is **not** "purification/light". Seris explicitly rules light out: it resembles fire more than anything, it is viscous, and it rises instead of dissipating. It has no name yet. Her cowl also doesn't "drain fast" once she stops fighting it — by Ch 20 it's the biggest and steadiest in the group.

Also: Leonard flies with wind magic (he uses air to smother fire around himself), so "apparently flies without one" should just read as wind-nature flight, the same as Severin's.
