# Character Voice Notes

What I took from reading the whole thing — 1st Revision for Ch 1–10, manuscript for Ch 11–25. This is my read, written down so you can tell me where it's wrong. Correct anything here and I'll work from the corrected version.

For each person: how they talk, what they do with their body, the tell that gives them away, and the lines that fix the voice. The quotes are verbatim.

The dialogue is yours and I don't touch it. What this sheet is actually for is the narration between the lines — who goes still, who fidgets, who looks where — which is the part I write and the part where I can get someone wrong while every word of your dialogue stays correct.

## The core six

### Varen

**Voice:** Shortest possible. Declarative. He answers the question actually asked and nothing around it. When he can't answer he says nothing — silence is his version of I don't know, and it's learned: Caen's patience for inaccuracy was zero. His biggest moments are three words long.

*“My food.”* Ch1 ¶21

*“I'll burn them.”* Ch1 ¶66

*“I am Varen.”* Ch3 ¶137

*“What's rent.”* Ch9 ¶180

*“Are you hungry.”* Ch14 ¶127

**The verb:** He files. It's the single most characteristic thing about his interiority and it recurs constantly — he receives information, declines to react, stores it. He files insults, pain, Caen's deviations, Mara's lies, the sound behind the compound wall, the extra wood on the fire. The file is where everything he doesn't understand goes to wait.

**Body before mind:** Repeatedly stated and it's structural, not a flourish. His feet move before his mind authorizes it. His body makes the call and the mind notes it afterward. He throws the knife to Mara, then wonders what answered for him.

**Behaviour:** Wraps his own injuries without asking and without being offered. Resets his own shoulder and doesn't make a sound. Does the work before the comfort — fire is a reward for finished work, never a condition of it. Doesn't look back at things he's left. Doesn't search. Doesn't ask Caen why, ever, in twelve years.

**Tells:** The hand that doesn't open. Eyes that settle rather than dart. He bites down until his gums bleed to locate himself in the present — self-taught, in the wolf's jaws, not something Caen gave him.

**Interior rule:** Fear is information, not an obstacle. He was afraid of the wolf and fought it anyway and learned from that that fear can be present and irrelevant at the same time.

**Where he's soft:** He can't read what people feel, but he notices what they do. The first lie of his life is to protect Mara's feelings and it arrives without the friction he expected. He clocks Ryn nudging on her behalf and then does the thing Ryn was angling for.

### Mara

**Voice:** Continuous. Not bursts — a stream that's found its channel. Stories chain by association, and something always ends up on a roof. She tells you how the dog got up there without being asked.

**The pivot:** When a question gets close she pivots without a seam, and the pivot is always a volley of questions back at you, or a generalisation delivered at speed so there's no gap to ask into.

*“All kingdoms look the same anyway.”* Ch10 ¶38

*“You know what, how long have you been on the road? … Did you kill a wolf? How old are you?”* Ch6 ¶146

**Her one-word weapon:** “Obviously.” Deployed when a question is faintly insulting and she needs it to stop.

**Anger:** Volume, then an instant shutdown. She goes to caps, clocks the heads turning, and the surface is back up inside a sentence. She never stays angry where people can see.

*“I'M FINE.”* Ch10 ¶103

**Where she's actually good:** Money, and she's authoritative about it. Her best speech in the first ten chapters is economics, not feeling.

*“It pays the most because it kills the most. That's how it works. The reward goes up every time someone dies. That's not a good job, that's a warning with a number on it.”* Ch10 ¶120

**Behaviour:** Positions herself beside, never behind, and takes uncomfortably long steps to hold the pace. Moves at height better than she does anything else. Counts coins in her sleep and giggles at the sums. Fabricates memories so completely she half-believes them.

**When she's honest she goes flat and short.** Out of practical arguments, she stops performing and says the true thing in six words.

*“I want to go with you.”* Ch10 ¶149

*“I'm going to get stronger. I won't be in your way anymore.”* Ch19 ¶285

**The gap:** She knows why the seven adventurers stayed and can't say it, because nobody has ever stayed for her. She understands it the way you understand a language you studied and never spoke.

### Ryn Casvel

**Two registers, one hard switch.** The pleasant one is warm, fluent, slightly too casual for the room. Underneath it is flat and accurate. The switch is the character — after he kills Voss the pleasant layer drops, he says the flat thing, and it snaps back “between one breath and the next, as if the dead expression had been a momentary malfunction.”

*“This is death.”* Ch7 ¶68

*“Fantastic. Nice to meet you. Sorry about the mess.”* Ch7 ¶77

**He needles with wrong names.** Corbyn tries to shoot him point blank and Ryn, without the smile changing, calls him Gerald.

*“Uh. Slow down, big guy. … Oh, you're a silly one, Gerald.”* Ch7 ¶95–97

**He counts and scores himself.** Constantly. Eight men when he estimated seven — he makes a note. Three more inside than expected — another note. The deal takes eleven minutes against an estimate of ten — another note. He is always marking his own predictions, and this is how you show him thinking.

**He doesn't negotiate a fair price, and says so when asked.** Vorne reads this as either foolish or already-won and files it.

*“The price is fair.”* Ch11 ¶373

**Behaviour:** Never performs surprise, never reaches for anything, never steps back. Plans for your worst move — the revolver he hands over is empty and his face doesn't move when the trigger clicks. Clean after a forest because of how he moves through it. Likes a full schedule and knows why.

**The real smile:** He has a performance smile and a real one, and the text always tells you which. The real one appears when something genuinely surprises him and concealing it would be a waste — Aldric's forge, Seris smelling the suevite.

**The fragments:** No architecture. A warmth, a colour, the weight of something held. Filed under “not now, not here, not useful.” He has noticed the file is thickening and the intervals shortening and has deliberately not decided what to do about either.

*“You're making that face again. You're thinking too much.”* Ch11 ¶102

*“What have you done to me.”* Ch21 ¶15

### Sera

**Voice:** Low, unhurried, warm, and it stays warm when it has no reason to. She thanks an innkeeper at the end of the worst day of her life. Ryn's read of her is that she's the kind of person who wouldn't shut a stranger down even when shutting them down would cost her nothing.

**The shift that is the whole character:** Her question changes from “is this your will” to “Where are you.” She says the first for months and the second once, on her knees in an alley, and the second has no floor under it.

*“Where are you.”* Ch12 ¶101

*“I want to know if my life mattered. … I want to know what her will actually is.”* Ch13 ¶158–161

**She is precise about what she rejected.** Not the goddess — the institution. She holds that distinction even while coming apart, and she keeps the habit folded in the chest rather than discarding it.

**Under pressure she gets shorter, not louder.** Her strongest line in the whole draft is three words, barely audible, after she has run out of bigger arguments.

*“And why not.”* Ch15 ¶219

*“Once I find the answer, then I'll worry about whether I like it.”* Ch15 ¶203

*“No one can erase a fairy tale.”* Ch22 ¶116

**Body:** Obeys before she decides to — Seris says sit straight, chin up, fix your collar and her spine is already doing it. Eighteen years of ambient institutional correction living below conscious response. Her hands pull toward the old motion and she has to stop them.

**Guilt is her default setting.** She apologises for taking up a chair. She reads the difficulty of healing magic as her own slowness rather than the difficulty of the thing.

### Caen / Signus

**He states, he doesn't ask.** Flat register that does not change when the content escalates. He reports the world's condition rather than enquiring about it.

*“You're hungry. There's food in front of you.”* Ch2 ¶25

**The question is the weapon.** He asks things he already knows the answer to, and the asking is the attack.

*“Oh. Is this a special place.”* Ch1 ¶40

*“Do you feel pity for him, little animal?”* Ch3 ¶107

**He wins by being correct in a way you can't answer.** This is the mechanism underneath the flat tone, and it's what makes him frightening rather than just cold.

*“He didn't do anything to me. / The wolf didn't do anything to you either. Until it did.”* Ch3 ¶73–74

**Caps are force, not rage.** He escalates volume without escalating emotion. “WHAT ARE YOU?” “DO YOU THINK A SNAKE LOSES ITS VENOM IF IT DOESN'T BITE ANYMORE?”

**He deflects personal questions with a question, and performs uncertainty when he's withholding.** Asked where he's been: “Why? Did you miss me?” Asked why Yharim did it, he says “I don't know,” then offers “Is it power?” — he knows.

**Authority is stated once and the room is over.** Three flat sentences and Lyris sits down.

*“Your concern is noted. Your report was complete. You may sit.”* Ch8 ¶51–53

**He refuses the frame rather than arguing inside it.** “The chain of authority does not pass through you.” “You are not the Devourer.” “The reasonableness was cosmetic.”

**Storytelling is his one register shift.** Ch 4 is the only place he sounds different — short placed sentences, one idea per line, building by accumulation, repeating a word until it lands. “He went in a boy. / He did not come out a boy.” Keep that register exclusive to him telling the Yharim story.

**The grin is the tell.** It appears only when something he has been building toward arrives. Three sizes: the wide one in the ruins, the quiet knowing one when he hands over the knife, and the one that “got bigger.”

**Warmth is only ever repetition with the contempt removed.** “little animal” first as a label, later with the hostility simply absent. He never says a warm thing directly, ever.

**Grief is physical and silent.** He unmakes the cabin and finds the smell of wood smoke the hardest detail to let go of. He sits in the cold ash and puts his hand in it. He does not look at the table where the sword was. Not one line of dialogue in the whole sequence.

### Seris Dahl

**Two words end things.** Her corrections are the shortest in the book and they are never softened. “That's enough.” “That's good.” “Well done.” “Again.”

**She calibrates per student, deliberately.** Validation for Bael because he needs to know he's right before he commits. Nothing at all for Myre because Myre grows by proving it unacknowledged. She knows exactly what she's doing and the text says so.

**She answers a question with the question underneath it.** “Why did you come here?” asked twice, the second time meaning something different. “Why do you want to find Providence.”

**She does not comfort, and she doesn't pretend to.** “Your faith is weak.” “That's right. You don't know.” “Regression is not allowed in my class.” None of it is cruel and none of it is softened.

**Her honest register is rare and it's quiet.** “I'm not the person to ask that. If you want an answer to that question, you'll have to ask her yourself.”

**Body:** The shoes announce her before she appears — she decided long ago that announcement beat surprise. She arrives exactly on time, not early, not late. She produces a water blade with no cowl, no preparation and no visible effort, and the absence of preparation is the point.

**The one crack:** The involuntary smile when Sera's cowl surpasses Bael's, erased almost fast enough — Myre catches it. And the excitement on her face at the Wand of Rime, which Emmeline immediately tries to trade on.

*“This stray could be a lot of things. Lost isn't one of them.”* Ch13 ¶193

*“Don't bring me down to your level.”* Ch23 ¶167

## Seris's students

They work as a unit and the comedy between them is load-bearing — it's where the book breathes. The rhythm is: Davan starts it, Solen plays straight, Bael takes it personally, Myre lands one flat line, Iren says the sincere thing.

### Davan Astorel  *(wind)*
**Jokes by escalation, not by quip.** This is the thing to get right. He takes a premise, builds a heroic frame, and collapses it on an absurd sensory detail. Two or three beats, never one.

*“Solen of the Western Brigade. The great cleanser. Mothers naming their children after you. Not because you saved the city. Because their children smell wonderful.”* Ch20 ¶366

*“A genuine briefing. On her own performance. Very thorough. Very helpful.”* Ch24 ¶409

**He pushes on the sore spot on purpose and it works.** When Solen is wounded, Davan goes straight at the wound and keeps going, and it releases rather than deepens it. Sera watches this happen and can't work out why.

**He is the first to move when someone is in danger.** He reads the pressure differential before Highmore releases and puts his body between Bael and it. He says no to Highmore's face with his knees shaking.

**His anger has one source:** Balthazar. He takes on a man who just cut ten soldiers apart because of a statue he walked past as a child.

### Bael Vorenthal  *(fire)*
**Flat assurance in everything, including his own name.** “Bael Vorenthal.” Delivered as a formality because he assumes the name means something.

**Heat arrives faster than he intends it to.** The text says so twice. It arrives before he's decided to be angry, and it's always the same sore place — whether he earned his position.

*“So what. You're saying that like it means something. Like we didn't go through everything we went through to get here. We earned our positions.”* Ch20 ¶72

**His cruelty is a jab at a seam he can see.** “I thought you nuns loved dying out there.” He finds the place and hits it, and Seris shuts it down in two words.

**He gives credit badly and means it completely.** Telling Myre her lightning was impressive is genuine pride in a rival — and he delivers it like a status report, which is what Davan then destroys him for.

**He recalibrates in public.** When Sera's cowl passes his, the smirk goes and he starts building a new category for her rather than resenting her. He competes; he doesn't sulk.

### Myre Telvaris  *(lightning)*
**Shortest speaker in the group.** Direct, no ceremony, no softening. “I've seen it.” “I'm aware.” “Come on.” “We don't have all day.”

**She deflects praise at speed.** “It was a first attempt.” The deflection arrives practised — she covers the receiving before the receiving becomes visible.

**She watches everyone, always.** Watching is how she locates herself. She catalogues what Seris gives Bael and what Seris gives her, and she sees the smile Seris erases.

**Her private wound:** Whether she earned her place. It predates Sera and Sera's arrival only sharpens it. She does not say this out loud to anyone.

**Her kindness is blunt and it works.** She reassures Sera by stating a fact about Seris rather than by being gentle — “She wouldn't have taken you in if she didn't see something worth taking.”

**Under threat she answers anyway, afraid.** “Yes we are.” “You're from House Highmore of Oakhaven.” Her voice is afraid and the words come out regardless.

### Solen Arenthios  *(water)*
**Easy, unhurried, above the fray.** Talented siblings, so he has no jealousy in him. He's the one who defuses Bael by not engaging — “We're just talking. Calm down,” with the half-laugh underneath.

**His one real wound in the draft is being scolded for the first time.** He has never been on the receiving end of Seris and it lands hard. His composure holds and then does something else.

*“I only know water magic. What else is it supposed to do.”* Ch20 ¶213

**He is practically kind without comment.** He turns Iren away from the bodies and holds her there while she's sick and neither of them says anything about it afterwards.

### Iren Thessaly  *(earth)*
**Processes aloud, carefully.** She thinks by speaking and the speaking is deliberate. She's the one who articulates what the group just learned.

*“It's not discouraging — it's clarifying. It tells you what the work is actually for.”* Ch22 ¶28

**Useful before she is warm, and that is how her warmth shows.** She tells Sera about the room, the washroom, the quartermaster — being useful is her way of being kind.

**She reaches for the reassuring thing and can't always find it in time.** Twice: she opens her mouth and nothing comes, and someone blunter gets there first.

**Physically the least armoured of the six.** She's the one who is sick at the sight of the soldiers, and the one Highmore picks as “the most feeble” in the Ch 26 notes.

## The Godless Hand

Six people who are genuinely comfortable with each other, which is what makes them work. They banter mid-fight. They are devoted and they are not solemn about it — except Elysia.

### Cedric  *(earth / quartzite)*
**He talks all the way through a fight and enjoys it.** Conversational register, delivered between attacks, and he is not performing — he genuinely finds the fight interesting. He uses Vorne's first name.

*“To be honest with you — I didn't want to meet you today, Aldous.”* Ch23 ¶25

*“I always liked to get up close and personal with the people I kill.”* Ch23 ¶119

**He compliments his enemies and means it.** “A brute who isn't simple. I'm almost surprised.” He measures before he fights, and when Vorne stops holding back, Cedric is satisfied rather than alarmed.

**The one time his voice rises is doctrine.** Not pain, not the broken elbow — the word innocent.

*“Ignorant you mean. All of you are scum who are unable to distinguish between good and evil without a doctrine.”* Ch25 ¶136

**Grief makes him stop finding it funny.** Kneeling in Silas's blood he reaches for the usual back-and-forth and it isn't there. “Stop being so sensitive. You're not dying from this.” Then the farewell, and then he kills everyone nearby.

*“Let the deep void claim you, brother. We will meet again in the eldritch.”* Ch25 ¶113

### Silas  *(light / summoning)*
**Dry, flat, economical. Everything is an expenditure he hasn't budgeted for.** “Perhaps we call for a round of ale.” “How can I say no, since you asked so nicely.” “You're in no position to be arrogant.”

**Pride overrides tactics and it kills him.** He takes the shot at Berrick because Cedric is watching, not because it's sound. The text says the pride arrives before the calculation.

**His life's work gets his only non-flat line.** The register changes exactly once, when he names the toad.

*“Sangui-batrachos. My magnum opus. All my life's work has led to this.”* Ch24 ¶191

**Curiosity survives everything.** Shot through the stomach he is still turning the rifle over: “This is not an artifact. What is it.”

**Last words are a joke at Cedric's expense.** “Fuck. I can't believe you're going to outlive me.”

### Aurelius Highmore  *(compressed air)*
**He talks too much and knows it.** “Here I go talking too much again.” He fills the room with conversation while the people in front of him wait to die, and he's aware of the effect.

**Courteous register, firm underneath, and the gap is the threat.** “Did you lose your etiquette. Introduce yourself. Please.” The politeness is the covering; the firmness is the real thing.

**One word gets through his calm.** Heretic. It finds something real every time, especially from a child.

*“I really hate that word. Especially when it comes from a child.”* Ch24 ¶312

**He is genuinely fond of young mages and genuinely murdered his teacher.** Both at once, and he doesn't experience the contradiction. He tells the story of killing Balthazar with tenderness and then tells the students to go home — twice — before he'd have to hurt them.

**What actually rattles him:** Davan pointing out that the murder achieved nothing. The jaw clenches. The hands that cut ten men apart without expression tighten.

### Emmeline  *(pure cold)*
**Clipped, impatient, and the most sceptical person in the group.** She is the one who says the plan is bad while executing it perfectly.

*“Pilgrims. What type of plan was this. / It's not that bad. / It's moronic.”* Ch23 ¶147–149

**She negotiates mid-fight.** She reads Seris's hunger for the wand instantly and tries to trade on it. That's her mode: find the lever, offer the deal.

**Precision as a personal habit.** The braid with not a strand out of place. The cold with no moisture in it. She is the tidiest person in the book.

### Elysia  *(fire)*
**She almost never speaks, and when she does it's doctrine.** Severin does the talking for the pair. She recites. Questions directed at her are answered by him or not at all.

**She recites over the dead and goes quiet afterwards.** Severin has learned not to interrupt it. This is the most human thing about her and it is also the most frightening.

**The tell:** Her eyes go wide and she stops blinking. It happens when the church robes appear and when someone says something clever. Watch for it — it's the only warning she gives.

**Her forehead carries the prayer, and the prayer is personal rather than doctrinal.** “Keep me and what I love from kneeling to what was stolen and called divine.” What I love. That's the whole character.

### Severin  *(wind, flight)*
**Cruel and amused, and the amusement is real.** He laughs through fights. He makes jokes about what colour robes should be. He is enjoying himself.

*“Those robes would look much better in red.”* Ch23 ¶406

**Pain has been renegotiated into a different category.** He works a crossbow bolt out of his shoulder joint and his face produces no reaction at all — the text is careful that this isn't stoicism, it's absence.

**He has one line of principle and he kills a man over it.** He is contemptuous of Leonard begging him rather than his goddess, and the contempt is genuinely offended.

*“Did you forget her name, or is it because at this particular moment you think she is inferior to me.”* Ch23 ¶443

**He is the one who's surprised by the clerics abandoning Leonard.** “And I thought the Hand were brutal.” Their theology didn't budget for that.

## Ordenmere

### Aldous Vorne  *(captain, western brigade)*
**Minimum necessary words, always.** “Enter.” “Show me.” “The log.” “Explain yourself.” “Now leave my sight.” He communicates in the fewest words that will do the job and treats directness as a form of respect.

**He thinks in questions, in order.** “Can you speak? Are you lost? Are you hurt? Do you need something here?” And when the answers don't produce a conclusion he sits with it rather than filling the silence.

**His best scene is him alone, reading.** The gate log reconstruction in Ch 22 is the character: thieves, no; Alister, no; they came from the north, that part's true; the lie is the gate. He gets to assassination by elimination, in silence, in his own head.

**Anger is dry.** “What a blessing the two of you must have been for the weary pilgrims.” “Let us pray in her name, for both your sakes, that this is a false alarm.”

**He files things, like Varen.** Different flavour — a man who hid nothing either had nothing to hide or had it hidden so well it didn't need concealing, and he'd determine which over time.

**He goes red whenever Seris addresses him and the whole compound has noticed.** He does not know this.

### Aldric  *(dwarf blacksmith)*
**Two or three words at a time, and they're complete.** “Morning.” “A hundred.” “When.” “I can make this.” The entire first transaction is six words from each of them.

**He says the important thing twice, flatly.** “It's designed to withstand a blast. I would know.” And again at the end of the same scene: “I would know.” That repetition is him, not an error.

**He shows rather than asks.** He introduces a single deliberate tolerance error to tell Ryn he knows what the part is for — then fixes it without being asked. That's how he opens a conversation.

**The warmth, when it comes, is about work.** “It was a pleasure. Working on a component like this. Intricate. Most people don't bring me intricate. Most people don't know what intricate is.” It's the longest thing he says in the book.

**He gives a warning like an insult, affectionately.** “What part of village taken by the rot are you not understanding. Was the price of all that cleverness your common sense?”

### Maret  *(supervising nun)*
**Flat, true, nowhere soft to land.** “It's time to return.” “There are no families.” She delivers hard facts without cruelty and without cushioning, because forty years have worn her to that surface.

**She admits the thing she's most ashamed of without being pressed.** That she never looked, and that the practical reasons were not the reason.

*“I was afraid. Not of what was out there. … I was afraid of the answer.”* Ch12 ¶309

**She reads Sera's face as her own from thirty years ago and says so.** Her kindness is accuracy — she tells Sera the truth about why her faith won't fill the gap, and frames the purse as an outstanding debt rather than charity so Sera's hands can accept it.

### Father Ignatius  *(73)*
**Deliberate is the whole technique.** He has decided deliberate is more frightening than fast and has been proving it for seventy-three years. He turns slowly so you experience every degree of the turn.

**Rage arrives complete and departs the same way.** Not a build. An arrival, like weather decided elsewhere. Teeth together, drool at the corner of his mouth, and then it's simply gone and he is conversational again. Everyone in the room has seen it before and knows to hold still.

*“Your failures to carry her will — are yours. And yours only.”* Ch23 ¶303

*“How dare those heretics sully her holy grounds.”* Ch23 ¶312

**The eyes do the work.** Pale cold blue, and the text uses them as the instrument — they give nothing back and ask for everything.

### Lise  *(16, novice)*
**Sincere all the way down, and she hands over her name against the rule.** “I'm Lise.” Then immediately: “When's the last time you said yours? Out loud?” She is the only person who asks Sera that.

**Her line is the worst thing in Ch 12 and she means every word.** “It must be beautiful to die doing Providence's will.” She says it quietly, in the afternoon light, hours before she sees Wenna's face.

**When it lands she says one word and goes inside.** “Wenna.” That's all. She holds very still inside herself, the way the ground taught them.

### Alister

**The pleasantness is the threat and Mara has understood this since she was six.** He never instructs and never threatens. He compares, and then he waits, and then he stands.

*“My other workers made this in an hour. / You were gone all day.”* Ch9 ¶268–270

**He puts weight on a single word to make her feel it.** “Working.”

### The knights  *(Tristan, Berrick, Lucian)*
**They barely speak and they're characterised entirely by behaviour.** Tristan gives orders that cost him — “Leave him” — and notes the cost and keeps moving. Berrick mercy-shoots his own man, and the man stops swinging so Berrick can do it. Lucian says nothing at all and positions himself correctly every time.

**Their best moments are refusals to stop.** Tristan loads a rifle with a left hand and broken fingers on the right, braces it on his thigh, and misses Silas by inches from sixty feet. Berrick, thought unconscious, gets up on his knees and kills Silas.

### Lieutenant Haes  *(northern brigade)*
**Listens, asks nothing, moves.** The rider delivers Vorne's message and Haes is already turning toward his soldiers before it finishes.

**Flat command voice that expects to be obeyed.** “Stop and identify your business past curfew.” “Hold.” “Release.” “Stay put. Nobody comes out.” He spends his last orders keeping other people inside.

**He dies kneeling with the sword and the shield still in his hands and the text never says anything sentimental about it.** Just the geometry of a man, the melted sigil, and one line: “Haes had not gone down without both of them in his hands.”

### Lyris  *(envoy)*
**Pleasant, professional, every sentence pre-calculated.** She attacks with a question and disclaims the attack in the same breath — “I raise this not as a challenge but as a concern. For the benefit of all present.”

**With a superior she concedes the uncomfortable thing cleanly and without hedging.** “That it isn't for the Devourer.”

**Her honest register only shows when she runs out of euphemism.** “You don't build something with a ceiling you can't read.”

**She chooses where to stand and when to leave.** Central without being conspicuous. Out in the middle of a group departure, never first or last.

**She is genuinely excellent and doesn't trust her own reading.** Recalibrates seven times and arrives higher every time.

**As a teacher she is good.** With Brask she is patient and specific: “Take more than feels safe. That's where the work actually starts.” “Direction comes after capacity, not before it.”

### The Weaver

**Short, curved, unhurried.** Orders in three words: “Find the boy. Observe. Report to me.” Questions with no question marks: “What do you know.” “Tell me.” “What did you conclude.”

**He closes a subject by renaming it.** “Signus is not your concern.”

**He threatens with the mildest phrasing available.** “We'll see about that.” “Don't be so sure.”

**When Caen finds the sore spot he loses the drawl entirely.** “Shut up.” “Shut your mouth.” Three times, each shorter.

**He thinks by moving. Always in a curve.** Stated twice, in two separate chapters. It's his signature in the narration.

## Patterns worth keeping

Things the book does consistently that read as deliberate, so I don't flatten them by accident:

• Two characters file things, and it means opposite things. Varen files what he can't understand. Mara files what she can use. Vorne files people. Ryn makes notes and scores his own predictions. Four different flavours of the same verb and they're all in character.

• Nobody explains themselves. Caen never explains, Varen never asks, Aldric says the thing twice instead of elaborating, Vorne reconstructs in silence. The book's default is understatement and the few times someone over-explains it's a character flaw — Highmore talking too much, Davan going too far.

• The villains are comfortable and the heroes are not. The Godless Hand banter mid-fight. Vorne, Haes, Tristan and Berrick barely speak. That contrast is doing real work.

• Repetition with the register changed. “little animal” twice. “I want to go with you” twice. “I would know” twice. “Highmore lowered his hand” twice. The second one always means something different from the first.

• Warmth never gets named. Caen feeds a fire and leaves. Ryn leaves food outside a door and says nothing. Maret calls a gift an outstanding debt. Solen turns Iren away and neither mentions it afterwards. Nobody in this book says a kind thing out loud.

*Tell me what I got wrong and I'll work from your version, not mine.*
