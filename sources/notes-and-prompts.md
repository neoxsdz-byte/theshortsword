# ch1-18

Ordenmere is a large, perfectly engineered kingdom that looks like a plus sign or a cross when viewed from above. Five equal square districts, each roughly 40 km², locked together wall to wall with no gaps. The West district on the left, the North district on the top, the East district on the right, the South district on the bottom, and the Capital sitting dead center where they all meet.

Hanging off three of the four outer diagonal corners of that cross are triangular extensions — the passage zones. Lower-left between West and South, lower-right between South and East, upper-right between East and North. The upper-left corner has nothing, just a wall. These triangles are dense cramped lower-class housing, the slums of Ordenmere, each about 20 km².

The entire outer boundary — the cross shape plus the three hanging triangles — is enclosed by a single thick heavily fortified stone wall with crenellations. Large defense towers sit at every outer corner. Smaller watch towers line every wall segment at roughly 100 meter intervals.

There are exactly three ways into Ordenmere. Two gates on the West district, one on the north wall of the west district and one on the west side of the west district. And the Bridge Gate on the eastern face of the East district, where the legendary stone arch bridge begins.

That bridge is enormous. It crosses the Great River — a waterway the size of the Nile — running along the entire eastern edge of the kingdom. The bridge is fixed stone arches all the way across except for one section right at the kingdom wall, which lifts like a portcullis. That liftable portion is the gate. The other bank is wilderness.

The Royal Road enters through Gate I on the west wall, winds through the West district, crosses into the South district, crosses into the East district, crosses into the North district, and finally enters the Capital through a grand ceremonial gate on the Capital's northern wall. Every visitor to the Capital must pass through all four outer districts first. That is not an accident. The kingdom itself is the security system.

The Capital has no windows to the outside world. No river access, no outer wall, no gate except the one leading from the North. Inside it sits the Royal Palace and the Church of Providence, the grandest cathedral in the kingdom. Each of the four outer districts has a smaller chapel built in the same architectural style as the Church of Providence — branch churches of the same faith.

The West district is the loudest and most chaotic — merchants, warehouses, market stalls, trade caravans coming and going through the two gates. The North district smells of smoke and hot metal — forges, workshops, kilns, the working class hammering things into existence. The East district faces the river — docks, piers, fishing boats, nets hanging to dry, barrels of salted fish. The South district is quiet and wide open — crop fields, barns, windmills, the smell of earth.

The three passage triangles connect the districts to each other at the corners, but they are not pleasant places. Narrow winding streets, buildings packed shoulder to shoulder, no grand architecture, no open squares. The people who live there are the lowest class in Ordenmere, squeezed into the leftover geometry of the kingdom's design.
